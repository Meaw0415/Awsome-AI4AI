# Positioning Against Preprints.org 202608.2108

## Paper

**AI4AI Survey: From Long-Horizon Agents to Recursive Self-Improvement—Definitions, Reliable Horizons, and Open Problems** (Wu et al., 2026).

## How that survey tells the story

The survey asks one central question:

> **How far can an AI system reliably carry an improvement process from idea to verified result?**

Its narrative is primarily **agent-era and reliability-centered**. The main structure is:

1. **Define AI4AI and closure** — target, self-reference, stage ownership, signal grounding, and evidence of improvement.
2. **Evaluate components vs integrated systems** — planning, memory, tools, coding, research, and integrated AI R&D benchmarks.
3. **Model-side interventions** — planning, tool learning, long-horizon credit assignment, and weight-layer AI4AI.
4. **Harness-side interventions** — execution, persistence, governance, and self-modification.
5. **Open problems by failure scale** — within one pass, across passes, across generations, and at the evaluation/control boundary.

Its most distinctive conceptual claim is the **composition gap**: strong isolated capabilities do not necessarily compose into a reliable end-to-end AI-improvement system.

The survey also proposes a useful harness progression:

`fixed human harness → externally optimized harness → self-modifying harness → model–harness co-evolution`.

## What we should borrow

### 1. Evidence discipline

For every AI4AI claim, ask:
- what exactly changed?
- who set the goal?
- who proposed and executed the change?
- what grounded the feedback?
- did the gain persist?
- did it transfer beyond the optimized task/metric?

This is especially useful for modern self-improvement claims.

### 2. Model–harness attribution

Modern agent performance should not be attributed only to the base model. Memory, context policy, tools, verification, rollback, search, and orchestration can materially change outcomes.

### 3. Composition gap

This should appear in our benchmark/evaluation synthesis: strong sub-capabilities do not imply reliable full-loop AI research.

## Where our review should be clearly different

### Difference 1 — We review the full historical AI4AI lineage

Their practical scope is the intersection of **long-horizon agents and AI-driven improvement**. AutoML and NAS mainly appear as predecessors or weight-layer examples.

Our review treats them as first-class stages of AI4AI:

`algorithm selection / HPO → AutoML → NAS → meta-learning / learned optimization → program & algorithm discovery → LLM optimization → agentic AI development → autonomous AI research → harness / improver optimization`.

This gives us a much stronger answer to **where AI4AI came from**.

### Difference 2 — Our main axis is the evolution of the improvement object and improver

Their main question is **how closed and reliable is the loop?**

Our main question is:

> **What part of AI development is being improved, how is it represented/searchable, what feedback drives the improvement, and when does the improvement mechanism itself become learnable or editable?**

This lets us connect Bayesian optimization, NAS, learned optimizers, evolutionary program search, LLM search, research agents, and recursive improvement in one design space.

### Difference 3 — We emphasize the methodological genealogy

Modern ideas often rediscover older concepts at a richer level:

- MLE agent search ↔ AutoML search, but over repositories/experiments rather than configurations.
- agent memory ↔ meta-learning / warm-start experience reuse, but over trajectories and research artifacts.
- harness optimization ↔ architecture/pipeline optimization, but the optimized object is now the agent runtime itself.
- recursive improvement ↔ learned optimization/meta-learning, but the improvement mechanism operates over much richer program/research spaces.

Our review should make these connections explicit rather than presenting the agent era as a separate literature.

### Difference 4 — Artifact space vs epistemic space

Classical AI4AI mainly optimizes **artifacts**: parameters, architectures, code, pipelines, metrics.

Research-level AI4AI also needs an **epistemic state**: hypotheses, uncertainty, evidence, contradictions, unresolved questions, and belief updates.

This supports a stronger forward-looking thesis:

`candidate → score → next candidate`

becomes

`experiment → evidence → belief update → changed value of future experiments`.

The recent preprint discusses reliability and composition, but this artifact/epistemic distinction can be one of our own conceptual contributions.

## Recommended macro-structure: six blocks

### Block I — Automated Optimization
HPO, algorithm selection, AutoML, resource-aware optimization.

### Block II — Automated Design and Learning to Improve
NAS, meta-learning, learned optimizers, population-based adaptation.

### Block III — Open-Ended Algorithm and Program Discovery
Automated objectives/rewards/data policies, AutoML-Zero, program synthesis/evolution, FunSearch, AlphaEvolve.

### Block IV — Foundation Models as General AI Improvement Operators
Prompt/text optimization, code generation, reward design, data/training-recipe generation, semantic search over AI artifacts.

### Block V — Agentic AI Development and Autonomous AI Research
MLE/data-science agents, data curation, post-training, long-horizon experimentation, AI scientists, reproduction/research benchmarks.

### Block VI — Improving the Improver
Workflow/agent design, memory/experience, harness optimization, self-modification, co-evolution, recursive AI4AI; followed by unified evaluation, artifact–epistemic synthesis, and open problems.

## One-sentence positioning

> **Where recent surveys ask how reliably modern agents can close an AI-improvement loop, our review explains how the loop itself evolved—from fixed-space automated optimization to systems that increasingly learn, redesign, and potentially improve the mechanism of AI improvement.**
